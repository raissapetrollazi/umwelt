# v0.3 Experiment - Behavioral World

## Status

This document defines the selected research direction for Umwelt v0.3. No v0.3
environment, model, intervention, result, or validation claim is implemented
yet. The experiment protocol and acceptance rules must be frozen in code and
tests before development-animal results are generated.

Version 0.3 will remain pre-alpha research software. Its purpose is to add one
narrow, explicit environmental dependency to synthetic movement and one
controlled computational intervention. It is not a general world simulator.

## Core question

> Does conditioning synthetic movement magnitude on an explicit, measured
> boundary zone reduce the path-length discrepancy of the v0.2 spatial baseline
> while preserving displacement and turning fidelity, and how does that same
> model respond to declared changes in arena size?

The first clause is an observational model-development question. The second is
a computational intervention question. An intervention response is a property
of the fitted model; it is not evidence that a real mouse would respond in the
same way.

## Why this experiment comes next

The v0.2 persistent reflecting random walk generated longer paths in all 32
replicates for both development animals. It used arena geometry only to reject
and reflect out-of-bounds movement. Its movement-magnitude distribution did not
depend on where the synthetic animal was in the arena.

Version 0.3 tests one additional assumption: movement magnitude may differ in a
geometrically defined boundary band and in the arena interior. This is a small,
inspectable environmental dependency motivated by a recorded model discrepancy.
It does not assume that boundary proximity caused the v0.2 discrepancy, and it
does not attach anxiety, preference, motivation, or another psychological
meaning to either zone.

The experiment reuses the Roche source and the v0.2 spatial contracts rather
than introducing a new dataset, modality, model family, and environmental
mechanism simultaneously.

## Source and development population

The source remains the Roche/ETH open-field Zenodo deposit
`10.5281/zenodo.8188683`, with the exact metadata, archive, extracted-file
manifest, coordinate frame, and provenance rules pinned for v0.2.

The analysis population remains the eight `Control` / dose `0` recordings. The
existing outcome-blind split is retained rather than changed after inspecting
v0.2 results. Version 0.3 changes how those roles are used so the two previously
inspected development animals do not become a model-tuning target.

Fitting animals:

- `16459-67074`

Legacy development-reference animals:

- `16459-67064`
- `16459-67078`

The six fitting animals form the v0.3 model-development pool. Model comparison
uses deterministic leave-one-animal-out cross-validation: each fold fits on
five animals and evaluates the sixth. After the complete protocol and model are
frozen, a final model is fitted on all six animals.

The two legacy development-reference animals have already been inspected in
v0.2. They may be evaluated once after the v0.3 protocol is frozen, but their
results cannot select parameters, change the model, or determine whether M1
advances. They are reported separately as an exposed diagnostic reference.
Version 0.3 therefore remains model-development evidence, not a prospective or
confirmatory test.

The Yohimbine recordings remain excluded: drug exposure is not an environmental
manipulation in this experiment and will not be used as a causal shortcut.

## Recorded spatial contract

Version 0.3 preserves the v0.2 recorded trajectory contract:

- use the source `bodycentre` keypoint;
- preserve source likelihood without inventing a cutoff;
- apply no interpolation or smoothing;
- do not bridge unavailable or nonadjacent frames;
- retain exact zero displacement;
- use the source `x-right-y-down` coordinate frame in image pixels;
- make no speed, physical-distance, or arena-size claim.

For each recording, the rectangular arena remains derived from the median
finite coordinates of the four source landmarks. A v0.3 arena-quality contract
must reject missing, nonfinite, inverted, or inconsistent bounds before a world
or zone is constructed.

## Minimal world contract

The first behavioral world contains only the state required by this experiment:

- a stable world and condition identity;
- the source coordinate frame and pixel unit;
- an axis-aligned rectangular arena;
- a dimensionless boundary-band fraction;
- an explicit intervention descriptor;
- the current world condition supplied to the movement model.

The boundary band is defined geometrically. For a point inside an arena with
width `w` and height `h`:

```text
clearance_fraction = boundary_distance / min(w, h)
boundary band      = clearance_fraction <= 0.10
interior           = clearance_fraction > 0.10
```

The `0.10` fraction is declared before v0.3 model results. It is dimensionless,
uses only validated image-space geometry, and remains identical across source
recordings and intervention conditions. Points outside the arena are invalid;
there is no third analytical zone.

The world has no food, shelter, reward, light schedule, social state, physics,
or hidden semantics. Those concepts require their own recorded question and
must not be added as generic infrastructure in this milestone.

## Model ladder

Version 0.3 compares exactly two models.

### M0: spatial baseline

`persistent-reflecting-random-walk-v1` remains the unchanged control. It fits
one pooled positive-displacement distribution and one pooled turning
distribution, then applies repeated-axis reflection at arena boundaries.

### M1: boundary-conditioned movement

`boundary-conditioned-random-walk-v1` changes only positive-displacement
magnitude. Within each cross-validation fold, every valid transition from the
five fitting animals is assigned from its starting position to the boundary
band or interior. The model fits one log-normal positive-displacement
distribution per zone.

The following components remain shared with M0:

- exact-stationary probability;
- turning-increment distribution;
- normalized initial position;
- boundary reflection rule;
- availability-mask handling;
- random-seed derivation;
- recorded trajectory extraction.

Each zone requires at least two positive fitting displacements to estimate the
declared log moments. Fitting must fail with an explicit data error below that
minimum and must not silently fall back to pooled or diagnostic-reference data.
This minimum is frozen before any v0.3 development run.

This ladder isolates one environmental dependency. A directional wall-turning
mechanism, zone-specific stationary probability, memory, goals, and learned
policies are outside the first v0.3 comparison.

## Arena-size intervention

After model comparison, the final M1 is fitted on all six development-pool
animals and simulated under three declared world conditions:

- `compact`: isotropic arena scale `0.75`;
- `recorded`: isotropic arena scale `1.00`;
- `expanded`: isotropic arena scale `1.25`.

Scaling is anchored at the recorded arena center and preserves aspect ratio.
The initial position is transferred by normalized arena coordinates. Frame
indices and the recorded `bodycentre` availability mask remain fixed across the
three conditions.

Each subject and replicate uses paired random streams across conditions. World
geometry is the only changed input: fitted movement parameters, model identity,
replicate count, and master seed remain fixed. Every generated observation must
carry its synthetic source category and world-condition identity.

The recorded-scale condition is the only condition compared with recorded
coordinates. Compact and expanded conditions are sensitivity analyses of the
model. They have no recorded counterpart in this dataset and cannot establish
real-animal causal effects.

## Frozen evaluation direction

The exact serialized protocol will be frozen before executing v0.3, but its
dimensions are declared here.

### Recorded-scale model comparison

Both models are evaluated across the six leave-one-animal-out folds using equal
frame grids, availability masks, seeds, and replicate counts within each fold.
The comparison retains the three v0.2 dimensions:

- signed relative path-length difference with valid-transition exposure;
- adjacent-displacement 1-Wasserstein distance in pixels;
- absolute-turning 1-Wasserstein distance in radians.

It adds two environment-linked dimensions:

- absolute difference in boundary-band occupancy fraction;
- zone-conditioned positive-displacement 1-Wasserstein distance, reported
  separately for the boundary band and interior.

Position-status counts, landmark stability, source-likelihood summaries, zone
sample counts, and valid-transition coverage remain quality control. They are
not silently converted into model-comparison scores.

The two legacy development-reference animals are then evaluated with final M0
and M1 models fitted on all six development-pool animals. Those results use the
same dimensions but remain a separate post-freeze diagnostic and do not enter
the preference rule.

### Pre-declared development preference rule

M1 becomes eligible for later independent evaluation only if all of the
following hold across the six cross-validation animals, using each animal's
replicate medians:

1. the median absolute path-length discrepancy across animals is lower than M0;
2. absolute path-length discrepancy is lower for at least four of six animals;
3. the median adjacent-displacement Wasserstein distance is no larger than M0;
4. the median absolute-turning Wasserstein distance is no larger than M0;
5. the median boundary-band occupancy error is lower than M0.

This rule is deliberately directional and strict, but it is not a hypothesis
test, validation threshold, or population claim. All component metrics and
predictive intervals must be reported even when the rule fails. A failure must
be retained as the result rather than repaired by changing the zone boundary,
folds, or metrics after inspection. Passing the rule does not promote M1 to a
validated replacement for M0; it only justifies testing the frozen candidate on
an independent source later.

### Intervention comparison

The intervention report compares compact and expanded conditions with the
paired recorded-scale simulation. It reports changes in:

- total path length with exposure;
- boundary-band occupancy fraction;
- boundary-band and interior transition counts;
- boundary-band and interior positive-displacement summaries;
- reflection counts.

No biological direction is declared as correct because the source has no
recorded arena-size intervention. The intervention must instead satisfy
computational invariants: deterministic replay from seeds, identical non-world
inputs across paired conditions, finite in-bounds positions, explicit condition
identity, and complete provenance.

## Information categories

Version 0.3 must keep the following categories explicit:

- **recorded:** Roche pose coordinates, likelihoods, landmarks, frame indices,
  and treatment metadata;
- **derived:** rectangular bounds, representative positions, movement steps,
  boundary distance, zone assignment, and evaluation measurements;
- **model state:** fitted movement distributions and current zone input;
- **intervention input:** condition identity and arena scale;
- **synthetic:** generated positions, movements, and condition-labeled
  trajectories.

Zone membership is a geometric derivation, not a recorded behavioral label.
The model's response to zone membership is an executable assumption, not a
recovered biological mechanism.

## Observer and model/debug views

The observer-facing record may expose position, arena geometry, zone identity,
frame availability, and intervention condition. A model/debug view may also
expose the distribution selected for a step, sampled displacement, proposed
heading, reflection events, and derived random seed.

Debug fields must not be presented as recorded measurements, and generating a
debug view must not change random-number consumption or simulation results.

## Reproducibility artifacts

A complete v0.3 run should retain compact artifacts rather than every synthetic
trajectory:

- `resolved-config.json`;
- `protocol.json`;
- `world.json`;
- `models.json`;
- `interventions.json`;
- `seeds.json`;
- `replicate-metrics.jsonl`;
- `model-comparison.json`;
- `intervention-comparison.json`;
- `provenance.json`;
- `report.md`;
- `artifact-manifest.json`.

Artifacts must record the source checksums, software revision and dirty state,
world and model identifiers, boundary-band fraction, arena-scale conditions,
split, master and derived seeds, information-category declarations, and hashes
of every retained artifact. Existing run directories must never be overwritten.

## Implementation sequence

Implementation should proceed in independently reviewable stages:

1. validate and serialize the minimal world and boundary-zone contract;
2. derive zone-labeled trajectory measurements with provenance;
3. implement and test the boundary-conditioned movement model;
4. freeze cross-validation, model-comparison, and intervention protocols in
   code;
5. implement paired world-condition execution and compact artifacts;
6. audit leakage, determinism, reporting language, and manifest integrity;
7. run and retain the canonical cross-validation and post-freeze diagnostic
   only after the previous stages are complete.

Each stage should preserve the existing v0.1 and v0.2 schemas and behavior.
Shared abstractions should be introduced only where this concrete experiment
requires them.

## Completion boundary

Version 0.3 is complete only when the repository can reproduce the full
recorded-to-synthetic loop at recorded scale, execute all three declared world
conditions with paired seeds, publish compact verified artifacts, and report
the pre-declared model preference rule without changing it after results.

Completion does not mean that M1 passes the preference rule. A reproducible,
well-measured negative result is a complete experiment.

## Non-goals

Version 0.3 does not provide:

- causal inference about Yohimbine or another treatment;
- anxiety, fear, preference, intention, motivation, or welfare inference;
- physical distance, speed, or time calibration;
- a general physics, game, reinforcement-learning, or artificial-life engine;
- researcher commands that directly puppet animal actions;
- food, shelter, reward, light, social, neural, or physiological mechanisms;
- general counterfactual branching infrastructure;
- graphics or a required renderer;
- confirmatory population evidence or biological model validation.

These boundaries keep the first behavioral world narrow enough to inspect and
make its assumptions, interventions, successes, and failures explicit.
