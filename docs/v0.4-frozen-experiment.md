# v0.4 frozen first social-model experiment

**Frozen before fitting or inspecting model outcomes.** This protocol follows
the [source audit](v0.4-source-audit.md). Its question is whether one explicit
resident response to the intruder's current position improves generated
pair-distance dynamics over independently moving animals. It tests a
computational dependency, not social intent or a causal effect in real mice.

## Data boundary and unit

Use only the verified CalMS21 Task 1 pose archive with MD5
`8a02654fddae28614ee24a6a082261b8`. The source's 70 training sequences
are sorted by SHA-256 of
`umwelt-v0.4-development-v1|<archive-md5>|<full-sequence-id>`; the first 56
in that order fit model parameters, and the remaining 14 form a development
set. This rule depends only on source identifiers and the published checksum.
The 19 source test sequences remain held out until this protocol, code, and
preference rule are fixed. Evaluate them once regardless of development
outcome; do not retune after either result.

The unit of evaluation is one recorded pair sequence. There is one resident
and one intruder within each sequence. The source claims an animal-identity
train/test split, and the exposed `mouseNNN` keys do not overlap. Intruder
identity across recordings is not independently available, so the report must
retain that limitation. Frames and synthetic replicates are not independent
biological pairs.

Use the source neck keypoint (index 3) in the 1024 × 570 image. Retain source
scores and every finite coordinate, without a confidence cutoff, interpolation,
or smoothing. A pair distance requires both neck points in one frame. Signed
distance change requires adjacent valid pair frames. The 50 px fitting rule
below does not remove observations from evaluation. Expert behavior labels and
task-programming features are excluded from fitting and evaluation.

## S0 and S1

Both roles use a pooled, image-space autoregressive movement component. For
role `a`, let `v[a,t]` be the two-dimensional neck displacement between
adjacent frames. S0 generates the next velocity as

```text
v[a,t+1] = alpha[a] * v[a,t] + epsilon[a,t]
epsilon[a,t] ~ Normal(0, sigma[a]^2 I)
```

The two random streams are independent. S1 keeps exactly the same `alpha`,
`sigma`, and role streams, but adds one term to the resident only:

```text
v[resident,t+1] = alpha[resident] * v[resident,t]
                    + epsilon[resident,t]
                    + beta * unit(intruder_position[t] - resident_position[t])
```

The unit vector is zero at coincident positions. `beta` may be positive or
negative; neither sign is declared biologically correct. This directional
drift can also affect movement magnitude. It is the sole difference between
the models. S1 does not observe the intruder's future position or any expert
action labels. The individual components are deliberately simple and are not
the failed v0.3 boundary model.

Fit each `alpha` by least squares of next velocity on previous velocity over
the 56 fitting sequences. Only triplets in which both consecutive role steps
are at most 50 px enter the estimator. This robust fitting rule was selected
from the pre-model training quality audit to limit the influence of extreme
tracking jumps; it is not a claim that all larger steps are artifacts. Clip
`alpha` to `[0, 0.98]`. Fit each `sigma` as the per-axis root mean squared S0
residual over the same eligible triplets. Fit `beta` as the mean projection of
the resident's S0 residual onto the current resident-to-intruder unit vector
over eligible resident triplets. Count and report every eligible and excluded
triplet. No parameter is tuned on development or test pairs.

For each evaluation pair, start both synthetic animals at that pair's first
recorded neck positions with zero initial velocity. If those positions are
unavailable, exclude the pair and report its identifier and reason. Advance on
the source frame grid, using the same observed availability mask for both
models and all replicates. Reflect coordinates at the 1024 × 570 **image**
limits. Those limits are a computational constraint, not inferred cage walls.
The pair's later recorded coordinates are used only to measure reference
distributions, never to drive the generator.

Generate eight replicates per pair. The master seed is `4042026`. Derive a
separate SHA-256-based seed from master seed, full sequence ID, replicate index,
and role. Each model uses the same per-role seed, so innovations are paired
across S0 and S1. Synthetic observations retain their synthetic source type,
pair and role identity, seed, model identity, and coordinate convention.

## Metrics and preference rule

For every pair and replicate, calculate exact empirical one-dimensional
Wasserstein distance in pixels between recorded and synthetic distributions of
(1) neck-to-neck pair distance and (2) adjacent signed pair-distance change.
Report valid position and transition exposure, recorded and synthetic
distribution summaries, and both discrepancies. Average each discrepancy over
the eight replicates within a pair, then weight pairs equally when calculating
the mean discrepancy for a split. Report all per-pair values, not only the
aggregate. Preserve empty or invalid outcomes explicitly; do not silently
drop them. A split-level preference is undefined if any eligible pair lacks
either metric.

Declare S1 preferred on a split only when all three criteria hold:

1. Its equal-pair mean distance discrepancy is at least 5% lower than S0's.
2. Its equal-pair mean signed-change discrepancy is no more than 5% higher
   than S0's.
3. Its mean distance discrepancy is strictly lower on at least 60% of
   eligible pairs (round up to an integer count).

Apply the same rule to development and test, report each criterion, and retain
a negative result. The test decision is the held-out result; a development
decision does not license changes to the test protocol. Neither decision
establishes biological validity, even if S1 is preferred.

## Reproducibility

Write the exact split IDs, model parameters and fit exposure, source checksum,
software revision and dirty state, seeds, pair metrics, aggregate criteria,
and artifact hashes. Keep raw and derived CalMS21 trajectories under ignored
local paths. A compact report may enter the repository, but raw poses or
reconstructed sequences may not. This use is limited to the non-commercial
research experiment described here; future commercial use needs a separate
rights decision.
